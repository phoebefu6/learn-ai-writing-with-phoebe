Your voice, captured

This is the highest-value forty-five minutes in the track, and it is not close. On the ladder the voice sample is worth +16 points - 52 to 68 - the biggest single jump there is, and you buy it with an artifact you build once and paste for the rest of your career. What you are buying is escape from the corporate default: without a sample the model reaches for utilise, leverage, facilitate and commencement, not because it is wrong but because that is the average of business writing on the internet, and averaging is what it does. Tonight we build the sample, look at the published evidence that convergence is the default, and measure your own real register in the lab.

Part 1 · the case

Why the voice sample is the big lever 11 min live

Look at the ladder again. Brief is worth 4. Structure is worth 2. Specifics, which cost you twenty minutes of finding out, are worth 13. Cutting is worth 15. And the voice sample - three paragraphs you already own, pasted in - is worth 16. It is the best trade in the whole course, and the reason is mechanical rather than mystical: the corporate default register is where most of the damage lives. It carries most of the passives and about half the AI-tells, so replacing it takes two of the seven measures down at once.

LiveWhat the default register actually is4 min▶

Ask for an update with no register given, and you get a very specific dialect: utilise rather than use, leverage rather than use again, facilitate rather than run, commencement rather than start, "in order to" rather than "to", and "it is worth noting that" in front of things that are not worth noting. Alongside that come the structural habits - the actor removed from the sentence, the verb turned into a noun, the closing paragraph that thanks the reader for their time.

The important thing is that none of this is a bug. A model produces the most probable continuation, and across the whole internet's business writing, that dialect is the most probable continuation. The default is not a mistake in the tool. It is an accurate reflection of the average, and the average is not what you want your name on.

Which gives you the cleanest way to think about the lever: you are not correcting the model, you are giving it a smaller target. Without a sample it aims at everyone. With a sample it aims at you.

LiveWhy two measures move at once3 min▶

When you watch 52 climb to 68 in the lab, two rows do almost all of the work, and it is worth understanding why they are linked:

  • Passive voice. The corporate register is passive by preference, because the passive is how you write about a decision without saying who made it. Your own sent writing is full of "I", "we" and "you", because you were talking to someone. Hand over the sample and the actors come back automatically - you never have to say "use the active voice".
  • AI-tell phrases. Delve, seamless, robust, landscape, furthermore, "it is worth noting". These are not errors, they are fingerprints of the average. A sample of your writing simply does not contain them, and matching a sample is a much stronger instruction than a banned list on its own.

Rhythm improves as a side effect, which is the sneaky third gain. Generated prose settles into a steady 18-to-22-word hum; your real sentences vary, because you wrote them while thinking. Rhythm is the most reliable tell there is and the one nobody can name, and a sample fixes it without anybody having to discuss cadence.

Self-studyWhy adjectives cannot do this job3 min read▶

"Write in a friendly, professional tone" feels like an instruction. It is not, in any operative sense, because the corporate default already satisfies it. Formal, polite, warm, clear, concise, confident - the default register is compatible with every one of those words, so specifying them changes almost nothing and you go away believing you gave direction.

A sample is different in kind, not degree. It carries information adjectives cannot encode: how long your sentences actually run, whether you use contractions, whether you open with the point or a greeting, what you do with a number, how you signal uncertainty, and how you say no. You could not write those rules down in advance for yourself, which is exactly why demonstrating beats describing.

Adjectives · changes almost nothing

"Write this in a friendly, professional tone. Keep it clear and concise, and make it sound human."

A sample · changes the register

"Here are three paragraphs I wrote and sent last month. [paste] Match this register: same sentence length, same use of contractions, same directness in the first line. Never use these words: utilise, leverage, facilitate, commencement, delve, seamless, robust, landscape, furthermore, it is worth noting. If a sentence in your draft could not plausibly have appeared in my three paragraphs, rewrite it."

That last sentence is the whole trick in one clause. It gives the model a test it can actually run against something concrete, rather than an adjective it can satisfy by doing what it was going to do anyway.

Part 2 · the artifact

Building the voice doc 9 min live

One document, two pages at most, built in about half an hour and edited maybe twice a year. Five parts, and each part exists to prevent one specific failure - which is the useful way to hold it, because it stops the doc growing into a style manual nobody opens. Build it tonight on the Cadence week: the update you owe the exec team about the two-week slip, the 48 samples and Thursday's decision is a perfect test piece, because it is short, real, and slightly awkward.

LivePart 1 · Collect five to eight paragraphs you would be happy to have sent3 min▶

Go into your sent folder and pull five to eight paragraphs of writing you are glad went out. Not documents - paragraphs. A tight explanation, a piece of bad news handled well, a paragraph where you said no cleanly, a paragraph where you were genuinely funny and it was fine.

The trap, and almost everyone falls into it: people systematically pick their most formal writing. It feels like the responsible choice, so they choose the board paper over the Tuesday email, and the board paper is where they were already writing in the default register. You then hand the model a sample of the thing you were trying to escape, and wonder why the lever did nothing.

The fix is a rule with a bite: at least half your samples must come from writing where you were not being careful. The Slack message that landed. The two-sentence reply that unblocked someone. That is where your actual register lives, and it is the register your reader recognises as you.

LivePart 2 · Annotate each one with why it is right2 min▶

One line under each paragraph, in your own words. "Says no in the first sentence and gives one reason." "Two numbers in three sentences." "Opens with what changed, not with hello." "Admits I do not know something without three hedges around it."

Two things happen when you do this. The obvious one: the annotation is instruction a model can use, and a much better instruction than any adjective you would have reached for. The less obvious one, and the reason this part is not optional: you find out what you actually do. Most people have never described their own register and are surprised by it - the sentences are shorter than they thought, the contractions are everywhere, and the openings are blunter.

LivePart 3 · Extract your rules2 min▶

Six rules, one line each, taken from the annotations rather than invented:

  • Person. Do you write "I" or "we", and when do you switch? Most people over-use "we" to spread responsibility, and it shows.
  • Contractions. Yes or no, and whether that changes with audience. Be honest - a "no contractions" rule is usually a formality reflex rather than a preference.
  • Typical sentence length. Not an average to hit, a range you actually live in, plus whether you use short sentences as punctuation.
  • How you open. The point, a greeting, or context. This single rule decides whether your drafts have a windup.
  • How you handle uncertainty. This is the one that matters most, and the model gets it wrong by default: it hedges. Write down your form. "We do not know X yet. Here is what would settle it, and by when."
  • How you say no. First sentence or last, one reason or none, alternative offered or not. b8 goes at the difficult cases properly, but the register decision belongs here.
LivePart 4 · Write your banned list2 min▶

Two sources, and you need both. The standard tells: delve, leverage, seamless, robust, landscape, tapestry, underscores, furthermore, "it is worth noting", "do not hesitate". Then the personal half, which is the useful one - open your own last three documents and find the words you reach for when you are padding. Everyone has four or five. Mine used to be "essentially".

Keep it short enough to obey. A banned list of sixty words is a document nobody applies, including you; twelve to fifteen items is a list a model will honour and you will remember. And keep it as a list of words, not a list of principles - "avoid jargon" is unenforceable, "never use utilise" is checkable in a second.

Self-studyPart 5 · Save it where you can paste from in four seconds2 min read▶

This sounds like an administrative footnote and it is the reason most voice docs fail. If pasting the sample takes a minute of hunting through folders, you will do it for the important pieces and skip it for the twenty small ones, which is where most of your writing time actually goes. Friction is what kills this habit, not doubt about whether it works.

So: one file, one place, plain text, no formatting to strip. A pinned note, a text snippet, a saved instruction in whatever tool you use - whatever gets it from your keyboard to the prompt in about four seconds. Then use it on something trivial today, so the motion is already automatic when the piece matters.

"Match my voice" · nothing to match

"Rewrite this to match my voice. You have seen my writing before, so just make it sound like me rather than like AI."

Three concrete markers · checkable

"Rewrite this in my register, which has three markers: I open with what changed rather than a greeting, my sentences run 8 to 18 words with occasional four-word ones for emphasis, and I state uncertainty plainly in one sentence instead of hedging across three. Use contractions. Say 'I' where I am the one deciding. Then list any sentence you were unsure about."

Part 3 · the evidence

Convergence is the default, and it is measured 8 min live

You do not have to argue this from taste. Three published studies point the same way, and each one closes off a different escape route: the default is more similar than a human crowd, better prompting helps, switching models does not, and something is lost that people can feel. Quoted as the papers frame them, and no further.

LiveThe default pool is more similar than a human crowd3 min▶

Meincke, Mollick & Terwiesch, "Prompting Diverse Ideas," arXiv:2402.01727. The measure is cosine similarity, where lower means more diverse:

Source of ideasCosine similarityReading
A group of students0.243Most diverse of the three
Chain-of-Thought prompting0.255Close to the human crowd
Base GPT-4 prompt0.377Markedly more internally similar

Two conclusions, both useful to a writer. A default model pool is markedly more internally similar than a human crowd - so the flattening is real and measurable, not a vibe. And better prompting closes most of the gap, which is the entire justification for the practice you are learning tonight. Direction works. The default is what you get when you decline to give any.

LiveSwitching models will not save you3 min▶

Wenger & Kenett, "Large language models are homogeneously creative," PNAS Nexus 5(3), pgag042, 24 March 2026. 102 humans against 22 LLMs across three divergent-thinking tasks. The models showed markedly lower semantic distance than the humans, with effect sizes of 0.87 to 1.8, and the finding held across model families.

That last clause is the one to keep. The most common reaction to flat output is to try a different model, and this is the citation that says the reflex is misdirected. It is not one vendor's house style; it is what averaging does. Which means the remedy cannot be procurement, it has to be input - your sample, your rules, your facts.

LiveAnd you feel less like it is yours2 min▶

Anderson, Shah & Kreminski, "Homogenization Effects of Large Language Models on Human Creative Ideation," C&C 2024, DOI 10.1145/3635636.3656204. 36 participants. Different users produced less semantically distinct ideas with ChatGPT than with another creativity-support tool, while producing more of them - and they felt less ownership of the ideas they produced.

The ownership finding is the one that matters in a writing course, because ownership is not a feeling you can afford to lose. It is step 5 of the loop. If you do not feel the piece is yours, you check it less carefully, you defend it less well in the room, and you learn less from having written it.

Real world

The practical conclusion, and it is a cheerful one. If the default output is the average of business writing, then sounding like a specific person is now a scarce good. Ten years ago your voice was a nice-to-have that a busy reader tolerated. Now it is the thing that makes your update readable in an inbox where six other updates arrived in the same register - and the whole cost of keeping it is one file you built tonight and paste in four seconds. Your voice is a competitive advantage precisely because the default is the average.

Build-along

Watch 52 become 68, then measure your own ★ 14 min · everyone runs it

The first box starts where b2 left us: brief on, no voice, 52 out of 100. Turn on Voice sample and watch it go to 68 - then read which rows moved, because that is the part that teaches. Passives and AI-tells do nearly all of it. After that, the second box: paste two paragraphs of your own and find out what your real register measures on the same seven checks.

Start with Brief already on: 52. Write down two numbers before you touch anything - the passive score and the AI-tells score.

Turn on Voice sample: 68, the biggest single jump on the ladder. Check your two numbers. Both moved, and you never gave an instruction about voice or about grammar.

Read the two drafts side by side and find the actors. In the 52 version decisions get reached; in the 68 version people make them. That is the whole 16 points.

Now the second box. Paste two of your own paragraphs - one from something careful, one from a Tuesday message. Compare the two scores, and notice which one sounds more like you.

Whichever paragraph scored better is the one that belongs in your voice doc. Most people are mildly annoyed by which one wins. Nothing leaves your browser.

What is written and what is measured The drafts are written examples - one good version, plus one specific kind of damage for each missing practice. Every number in the scorecard is computed from the text above it: hedge and AI-tell counts against real word lists, passive hits by pattern, sentence-length spread, nominalisation density with a whitelist so ordinary words like "evidence" and "institutions" are not punished, a real Flesch-Kincaid grade with syllable counting, and the share of sentences carrying a number or a name. The passive check is a heuristic - a be-verb followed by a participle - so read its count as a flag, not a census. And the score cannot tell you whether the writing sounds like you; only you can, which is why the second box asks for your own paragraphs rather than grading them.
Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 35-45 min total

  • Build the voice doc: five to eight paragraphs, one annotation each, six rules, a banned list of twelve to fifteen items, saved somewhere you can paste from in four seconds.
  • Test it on one real email you owe someone today. Draft with the sample pasted, draft without, and send whichever one you would defend in the room.
  • Audit your own selection: did at least half your samples come from writing where you were not being careful? If not, you picked your most formal writing and the exercise is defeating itself. Swap two.
  • Give a colleague three paragraphs - two of yours, one from a model with no sample given - and ask them to pick yours. If they cannot, your voice doc is not describing you yet. Bring the result to b4.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · The voice lever is worth +16, the biggest jump on the ladder. Which two rows do most of that work?

The default register is passive by preference, because the passive is how you describe a decision without naming who made it, and the tell phrases are fingerprints of the average. Replace the register and both rows fall at once. Note that a sample supplies no facts at all - that is slot 4, and it is a separate 13 points.
